Abstract

Nanobots are tiny machines created to carry out tasks on a nanoscale within the human body, these robots have a potential to deliver targeted drugs therapies, perform minimally invasive procedures, and improve efficiency in treatments for diseases such as cancer and diabetes. There are four main types of nanobots that are powered through various methods. Despite their promise they face challenges due to the excessive cost, immune response, and current materials available. However, with ongoing investigations and advancements in technology many issues are slowly being solved. With more research and funding nanorobots could transform modern healthcare providing new, less invasive treatments for diseases.

Introduction

Nanorobots or nanobots are extremely small robots typically 50-100nm wide. They are currently being assessed to carry out tasks and procedures on human bodies on a nanoscale that doctors will not be able to complete, enabling medical innervations that would be impossible or too invasive for doctors to perform. By using nanobots procedures will not leave as big of a scar and have faster recovery times. Doctors and scientists hope these bots will begin to inhabit our bodies as early as 2030.

Types of Nanorobots

Nanobots fall into one of four categories:

Cars - Nanorobotic ‘cars’ are the most advanced nanodevice to date. They can move using chemical and electromagnetic catalysts however the motors that drive the cars need to be controlled to steer the car.

Motors - Motors use the energy created from the change in movement of the molecules around them.

Shuttles - Shuttles can transport specific drugs to targeted areas in the body. Diseased areas usually have specific chemical or Ph markers so when the shuttle senses the corrected area it knows to release the drug.

Switches- Nanobot switches are either in an ‘on’ or ‘off’ state, they can be affected by heat, chemicals, magnetic fields, or enzymes. , M. Mehta, K. Subramani, in Emerging Nanotechnologies in Dentistry, 2012

Typically made from carbon-based materials in a diamond structure, they are durable and lightweight which gives it the strength to move around our bodies. They also have a super smooth surface area which will make it less likely to be attacked by antibodies.

How do Nanorobots navigate

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) devices are used to track and position nanobots. They are coated with biocompatible materials to ensure they do not cause toxicity in the body and ensure they can be easily detected in an MRI scanner. , Huaijuan Zhou, Carmen C Mayorga-Martinez, Salvador Pané , Li Zhang §, Martin Pumera , 2021 Once in the body, an external magnetic field generated by the MRI scanner will navigate the nanobot around the body.

How are Nanobots powered

There are many ways a nanobot is powered and more ways are still being created now most fall under one of three categories:

Self-propelled machines - self-propelled machines have been made in various shapes including spherical, conical, tubular, helical or asymmetrical geometries.

Fuel-propelled motors - Fuel-propelled motors operate when a particular fuel or chemical produces the energy needed to start moving.

Fuel-free propelled motors - Fuel-free propelled motors work through the energy created through magnetic fields or ultra sonic symbols.

Nanobots can be powered by generators and capacitors., Jyoti Ahlawat MS, Mahesh Narayan PhD, 2020 . Generators on board the nanobot use electrolytes found in the blood and are used as a chemical catalyst that when combined with the nanobots creates the internal energy needed for the nanobot to work. For external power, a small fiber optic wire is attached to the device. Then pulses of light are sent through it, these pulses of electricity power can the nanobots. Additionally, magnetic fields can be used to induce electrical currents using a closed conducting loop contained within the nanobots. Ultrasonic symbols are also used, employing a material called a piezoelectric membrane, which collects ultrasonic waves and transforms them into electrical power. Daniel Nelson, 2020

Problems and challenges faced

Nanobots are becoming more popular in the medical field but that does not come without challenges. They are expensive to build/research and the materials needed are not available now. Other challenges are with the human body itself attacking the nanobots, the immune system is designed to attack any foreign bodies to protect us and when injecting nanobots they get attacked and destroyed before carrying out their task, however doctors have found ways to reduce this but it is an ongoing problem that is currently being solved. Another problem faced is the nanobots getting affected by gravity. The term micro-g is a synonym for weightlessness or zero-g, indicating there is no gravity; however, there is a gravitational force acting on it, it is just small. This force, even though it is small, is still strong enough to affect the way nanobots travel in our bodies. , Melissa Palma-Jiménez, Yendry Corrales Ureña, Carlos Villalobos Bermúdez, José Roberto Vega Baudrit
